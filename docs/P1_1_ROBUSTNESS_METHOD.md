# P1-1 continuous robustness and thermal-motion method

## Status and scope

The P1-1 bounded calculation has now been run against the frozen P0-4
numerical-reference Hamiltonian.  The machine-readable result is
`data/forster_p1_1_robustness.json`, generated by
`scripts/evaluate_forster_p1_1.py`.  It contains a 37-geometry response grid,
independent P0-4 retained-block holdouts, a nested 128/256/512/1024-point Sobol
search, twelve local adversarial starts, and direct retained-block checks of the
three leading local candidates.  The
thermal part remains a literature-based scale benchmark rather than an
apparatus-specific gate-fidelity prediction, for the reasons stated below.

The existing 19-geometry/76-scenario validation and 32-point Sobol check are
finite sampled checks.  They do not establish the minimum over the continuous
position and Rabi-amplitude domain.  P1-1 is intended to (1) measure convergence
of increasingly dense deterministic samples, (2) search locally beyond the
sampled points, and (3) distinguish bounded engineering tolerances from a
probabilistic thermal-motion model.

The physics-independent implementation is in `scripts/p1_robustness.py`; its
unit tests do not import PairInteraction.

## Bounded continuous-robustness workflow

The uncertainty point is

\[
x=(\delta x,\delta y,\delta z,s_{\mathrm{Yb}},s_{\mathrm{Rb}}),\qquad
\lVert\delta\mathbf r\rVert\leq 50\ \mathrm{nm},\qquad
s_{\mathrm{Yb}},s_{\mathrm{Rb}}\in[0.99,1.01].
\]

`sobol_scenarios` generates a deterministic, unscrambled, five-dimensional
Sobol design.  For a unit-cube point \(u\), the spatial coordinates are mapped
uniformly in ball volume using

\[
r=50\ \mathrm{nm}\,u_0^{1/3},\quad
\cos\theta=2u_1-1,\quad
\phi=2\pi u_2,
\]

and \(u_3,u_4\) are mapped affinely to the two continuous amplitude intervals.
This improves on the current two-dimensional radius/direction-cosine sample
combined only with amplitude vertices.  Keeping Cartesian coordinates also
allows a later non-axisymmetric Hamiltonian or polarization model to use the
same scenarios.

The evaluator generates a single \(2^{10}=1024\)-point design once and evaluates
its prefixes at 128, 256, 512, and 1024 points.  The output records the minimum
fixed-correction fidelity and its full five coordinates at each prefix, with
the nominal point evaluated as a separate anchor.  `nested_minimum_trace`
implements this accounting.  The sampled minimum keeps decreasing because the
limiting axial boundary occupies negligible five-volume; the separate local
search, rather than the Sobol minimum alone, identifies and directly checks
that boundary.

The twelve lowest-fidelity Sobol points selected by `worst_sample_seeds` seed
`local_adversarial_search`.  It uses SLSQP with the exact ball constraint and
continuous amplitude bounds.  The search objective is a cubic tensor-product
interpolant of direct P0-4 retained-block grid evaluations.  Six off-grid points measure the
surrogate error, and the leading local candidates are re-evaluated with the
same P0-4 settings.  The defensible description remains **sampled minimum plus
local adversarial refinement**, not a certified global minimax value.

The direct minimum is \(F_{\mathrm{avg}}=0.9980009927\), at the same
\(+50\,\mathrm{nm}\) axial boundary with Yb scale 1.01 and Rb scale 0.99 as
P0-4.  The maximum absolute error on the six independent interpolation
holdouts is \(1.55\times10^{-5}\); the surrogate therefore guides the search
but is not used for the headline value.

## Thermal phase-space model

For species \(a\) and trap axis \(i\), `harmonic_phase_space_widths` uses the
thermal harmonic-oscillator Wigner distribution,

\[
\sigma_{x,a,i}^2=
\frac{\hbar}{2m_a\omega_{a,i}}
\coth\!\left(\frac{\hbar\omega_{a,i}}{2k_B T_a}\right),\qquad
\sigma_{v,a,i}^2=
\frac{\hbar\omega_{a,i}}{2m_a}
\coth\!\left(\frac{\hbar\omega_{a,i}}{2k_B T_a}\right).
\]

The classical option uses
\(\sigma_x^2=k_BT/(m\omega^2)\) and
\(\sigma_v^2=k_BT/m\).  Yb and Rb are sampled independently.  If both tweezers
are extinguished during Rydberg excitation, the relative trajectory is

\[
\mathbf R(t)=\mathbf R_0+
(\mathbf x_{\mathrm{Yb}}-\mathbf x_{\mathrm{Rb}})+
(\mathbf v_{\mathrm{Yb}}-\mathbf v_{\mathrm{Rb}})t.
\]

`ballistic_relative_trajectory` constructs this array, while
`doppler_detuning_mhz` computes \(-\mathbf k_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\cdot\!\mathbf
v/(2\pi)\) for each species.  `rotate_phase_space` rotates each species from its
trap principal axes into the gate coordinate system before propagation.  This
matters because the weak optical axis can have a much larger position spread
than either radial axis.

This thermal ensemble is a probability distribution and must be reported with
its seed, sample count, temperatures, trap frequencies, axis rotations,
wavevectors, and trap-off timing.  It is not a replacement for the bounded
50-nm stress test, and the bounded Sobol design must not be described as a
thermal distribution.

## Experimental parameter extraction

| Isotope/platform | Temperature | Trap frequencies | Provenance and use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(^{171}\)Yb Rydberg gate | \(T=2.9\,\mu\mathrm K\) during the gate | Appendix C quotes radial \(60\,\mathrm{kHz}\), axial \(10\,\mathrm{kHz}\) | Peper *et al.* report a \(1.4\times10^{-3}\) Doppler contribution at this temperature.  Their pair spectroscopy separately states \(T\approx3\,\mu\mathrm K\), predicts approximately 50-nm pair-distance fluctuations from these trap frequencies, and uses a 100-nm normal width to reproduce one broadened spectrum.  This is the direct Yb baseline [Peper2024]. |
| \(^{87}\)Rb single tweezer | release--recapture \(13(1)\,\mu\mathrm K\) after PGC; radial sideband \(11(3)\,\mu\mathrm K\); carrier dephasing \(16(2)\,\mu\mathrm K\) | measured \((f_{x'},f_{y'},f_z)=(154,150,30)\,\mathrm{kHz}\) | Kaufman *et al.* measured all three frequencies and three consistent PGC thermometry values.  Use \(13\,\mu\mathrm K\) only as a complete, cross-platform Rb benchmark until the intended Rb apparatus is characterized [Kaufman2012]. |
| \(^{87}\)Rb Rydberg array | \(5\,\mu\mathrm K\) immediately before Rydberg excitation | measured radial \(f_r=123\,\mathrm{kHz}\) at \(0.84\,\mathrm{mK}\) trap depth | Zhang *et al.* measured \(f_r\) by parametric heating, then lowered the trap to \(84\,\mu\mathrm K\) and switched it off for Rydberg excitation.  They do not report the axial or lowered-depth frequencies, so these two numbers do not define a complete same-condition 3D profile [Zhang2025Cryogenic]. |
| \(^{87}\)Rb--\(^{174}\)Yb dual species | Rb approximately \(30\,\mu\mathrm K\) after PGC; Yb approximately \(8\,\mu\mathrm K\) in the final MOT (main text says approximately \(10\,\mu\mathrm K\)) | no measured in-tweezer frequencies | Weber *et al.* demonstrate the closest dual-species platform, but it is \(^{174}\)Yb and supplies loading-stage temperatures plus calculated tweezer depths/waists, not a gate-time thermal profile.  Do not use it as the final P1-1 input [Weber2026]. |

The code interface accepts ordinary cyclic trap frequencies
\(f_i=\omega_i/(2\pi)\) in kHz.  Peper *et al.* denote the quoted values by
\(\omega_r\) and \(\omega_z\) while calling them trap frequencies; the proposed
input records them as 60 and 10 kHz, consistent with their approximately 50-nm
radial pair-distance estimate.  This convention must remain explicit in final
metadata.

For scale only, the seeded \(2^{18}\)-sample benchmark combines the direct Yb
baseline \((T;f_x,f_y,f_z)=(2.9\,\mu\mathrm K;60,60,10\,\mathrm{kHz})\) with
the complete but cross-platform Rb PGC benchmark
\((13\,\mu\mathrm K;154,150,30\,\mathrm{kHz})\).  These inputs give quantum
one-sigma position widths of (32.7, 32.7, 189.2) nm for Yb and
(36.9, 37.9, 187.1) nm for Rb.  Independent relative widths are therefore
(49.3, 50.1, 266.1) nm in aligned trap axes.  Over 360 ns of free flight, the
relative velocity width adds about 13.6 nm per axis, giving total widths
(51.2, 51.9, 266.4) nm.  At the target midpoint, 4.11% of the sampled relative
offsets lie inside the 50-nm bounded domain and the median offset is 193 nm.
The weak-axis Doppler standard deviations are 0.039 MHz for the Yb 302-nm
drive and 0.028 MHz or 0.119 MHz for counter- or co-propagating Rb 780+480-nm
beams.  These are cross-paper scale estimates—not a gate simulation and not a
coherent set of apparatus parameters.  They show why
the pair-axis/trap-axis alignment must be specified: approximately 50 nm is
reasonable in the radial plane but not along the weak optical axis.

## Inputs still required for an apparatus-specific thermal gate calculation

1. Trap frequencies measured at the release depth for each species, gate-time
   temperatures, and the two trap-axis rotations relative to \(\mathbf R_0\).
2. Rb two-photon and Yb single-photon effective wavevectors for Doppler shifts.
3. Exact trap-off/on timing and a time-dependent geometry evaluator (or a
   convergence-tested interpolation of P0-4 pair models along \(\mathbf R(t)\)).
4. A declared policy for fixed nominal calibration versus per-scenario phase
   recalibration.  The robustness headline should use fixed nominal correction.

Until these inputs exist, no apparatus-specific thermal gate fidelity should be
quoted.  This limitation does not affect the bounded P1-1 minimum above, which
uses the frozen P0-4 static domain and fixed nominal calibration.
